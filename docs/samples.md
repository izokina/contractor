# Sample Inputs

Small hand-verifiable inputs encoded as entries in `scripts/sample-fixtures.json`, ordered from simplest to most complex. Each entry has a `name` (the identifier used in the tables below), an `input` ExpressionJSON tree or `input_raw` text piped verbatim (for layout, escapes and malformed JSON), and an `outputs` array of expected results, or `"error"` holding part of the expected error message when the input must be rejected (exit status 1). An entry with `"permute": true` also runs with its root `Plus` summands reversed and shuffled. Verification runs through `scripts/check-fixtures.py` (driven by `bash scripts/run-tests.sh`, which builds the binary and a `-race` build and runs the script on both), piping each input into the contractor 10 times at `-threads 1`, 10 times at `-threads 8` and once at each through the race build, then all of it again with `-merge-memory=1`.

Tables with an **Output** column describe samples whose actual output must match that value exactly, apart from whitespace: JSON types included, and order at every level, since the output order comes from content alone (see [architecture.md](architecture.md) §Output order). The root `Plus` lists the pair-free monomials first, then the tensor terms by their Pairs. A success exits 0 with empty stderr, a rejection exits 1 with nothing on stdout, and stderr stays ASCII.

Samples are organised into topical sections below; numbers are not strictly consecutive — each section's range is listed below. Sections: contraction mechanics (01–07, 67–69, 107–108, 110, 119, 123–129, 169), parser behavior (08–18, 51–58, 64–65, 103–105, 115, 117, 120–122, 130–150, 160, 170–171, 179–195, 400, 625–628), coefficient folding (19–22, 62, 72–73, 111–112, 118, 151–155), scalar arithmetic (23–35, 41, 43, 46–47, 59–61, 63, 99, 102, 113, 116, 297–311, 319), exact large-magnitude arithmetic (42, 45, 48–50, 91–98, 100–101, 114, 156–159), output structure (36, 70–71, 106, 172–178, 196, 240–241), combinatorial (37–39, 66, 109, 197–198, 244–250, 290–296, 312–318, 320–322, 460), intentionally opaque (40), known implementation flaws (44), FeynCalc internal-notation contractions (74–90, 163–168), dimensions (161–162, 220–239), momentum expansion (199–207, 209–219, 242–243), merger subtrees and temporary files (360–370), exponents (480–502).

Rows that use `MT`, `FV`, `SP`, `MTD`, `FVD`, `SPD`, `MTE`, `FVE` or `SPE` follow the shorthand defined in [FeynCalc internal-notation contractions](#feyncalc-internal-notation-contractions).

## Scalar arithmetic

`Rational[a, b]` and `Complex[a, b]` are folded into the coefficient ring whenever their arguments are integers (of any magnitude — the ring promotes to `math/big` past int32) or, for `Complex`, integer-or-Rational real/imag. ## Exact large-magnitude arithmetic

Coefficient arithmetic is exact at any magnitude (§6): the int32 fast path promotes to `math/big` (`big.Rat`) when a result exceeds int32 and demotes back when a later result fits. ## Dimensions

An index or a momentum is 4-dimensional (no label), `D`-dimensional or `D-4`-dimensional (FeynCalc's evanescent dimension, written `["Plus", -4, "D"]`; shorthand `MTE`, `FVE`, `SPE`). Indices match by name alone. When the two sides of a contraction differ in dimension, the result takes their meet: `D` with 4 gives 4, `D` with `D-4` gives `D-4`, and 4 with `D-4` gives nothing, so the term is 0 and is dropped. The Pair a contraction makes carries the meet in both slots, and a metric traces to the trace of its meet: `4`, `"D"` or `-4 + D` ([feyncalc-notation.md](feyncalc-notation.md) §One index name in two dimensions). Rows 119 and 125 show `D` with 4; the rows below give the rest of the table. A Pair whose two slots carry different dimensions is rejected: 235–237 here, with 187, cover the mixes with `D-4`, as 06, 123 and 124 do 4 with `D`.

## Momentum expansion

A `Pair` is linear in each slot. The parser reads a `Pair` that the walk reaches, and that holds a `Momentum` whose source is a linear combination of momentum labels with exact numeric coefficients, as the sum of the Pairs of its labels, each with its coefficient; a scalar product of two combinations, as the product of both expansions. Nothing merges or cancels there: the walk, the contraction and the merger see each term. Anything else in a source is one opaque label, and nothing expands inside an opaque factor or a momentum source ([known-limitations.md](known-limitations.md) §Momentum sources).

## Merger subtrees and temporary files

The merger keeps its terms in subtrees by the first member of their words in content order, a term's first Pair or the end of its Pairs, and below that each monomial's opaque factors, and flushes the subtrees in member order; with `-merge-memory` it moves subtrees to a temporary file and merges them again at flush, one position deeper ([architecture.md](architecture.md) §Component Invariants > Merger). The output is the same either way: these rows pin the shapes of the path through the files, which the second test pass, `-merge-memory=1`, takes for every row. `g(a,b)` is the 4-dimensional metric `Pair[LorentzIndex a, LorentzIndex b]`, and every row but 369 and 370 runs in every summand order (`permute`).

## Exponents

Below the int32 bound a monomial holds each opaque factor once, with a positive int32 exponent ([scalar-normal-form.md](scalar-normal-form.md) §2): exponents add as monomials multiply, a power of one monomial is raised at once, and the writer writes an exponent `n` above 1 as `["Power", x, n]`. In the output order `x^n` ranks as `n` copies of `x`, so writing a run of equal factors as one `Power` moves nothing ([architecture.md](architecture.md) §Output order). `M` is `2147483647`, the largest int32.
