# Architecture — FeynGrav Index Contractor

Companion to `CLAUDE.md`'s Critical Facts. The briefing there is one-line per invariant; this document carries the structural reasoning, data flow, and per-component invariants behind those one-liners.

---

## Pipeline

```
stdin JSON
    └─► parse: codec.Parser, resumed summand by summand through iter.Pull; one worker at a time
            └─► items queue: batches of root summands
                    └─► walk: the worker's walk.Walker, pulled one term at a time
                            └─► the worker's batch of terms, then the batches queue
                                    └─► contract: a worker's Contractor, then its merge.Input (fan-in, one lock per batch)
                                            └─► Flush → Writer → stdout JSON
```

`worker.Run` starts `-threads` workers, and every stage runs on them, parsing included. A worker takes the earliest work available: a turn at parsing if the items queue has room, else a batch of summands to walk, else a batch of terms to contract, else its own unfinished batch; with none of these it blocks in one `select` over the parse token and the two queues.

- **Parsing is serial.** `Run` turns `ParseJson`'s callback into a pull with `iter.Pull`, and a token, the one value in a channel of capacity 1, admits one worker at a time. A worker outside a walk parses while the items queue has room; one in the middle of a walk parses one batch and returns to it. Only the holder sends on the items queue, after checking for room, so it never blocks there. At the end of input it closes the queue and keeps the token.
- **Batches.** A batch of summands closes once they are expected to yield `batchSize` terms (by `Len`, each counting at least 1). A walk appends its terms to the worker's own batch; a full batch goes to the batches queue, or, when the queue is full, the worker contracts it itself. Each queue holds a few batches (`itemsBuffer`, `batchesBuffer`), which bounds memory, since a summand is held whole until it is walked. `Walker.Next` and `ContractAndNormalize` return terms valid until their next call, so the worker copies each term into the batch's own slices. A contracted batch is cleared and goes on a free list that every worker takes its next batch from.
- **Termination.** A worker blocks only with its own batch contracted, and stops producing only once the items queue is closed and drained. The last to stop closes the batches queue (`producers`), and every worker drains it before it returns, so every term reaches the merger before `Run` returns.
- **Errors.** `fail` keeps the merger's first error, which only the temporary file of `-merge-memory` can raise; `Run` returns it, or else the parser's. Either stops all work: the parser stops at its next summand and closes the items queue, a walk stops at its next summand or full batch, and queued work is dropped. The parser sets its error in the coroutine before the items queue closes, so `Run` reads it after `wg.Wait` without a race. After a merger failure `Run` calls the iterator's `stop`, so that `ParseJson` returns without reading further; `main` then removes the temporary file (`Merger.Close`) and exits 1.

`Parser`, `Walker` and `Contractor` carry no mutex — the contract is *each instance is owned by one goroutine at a time*. A Walker, a Contractor and a `merge.Input` belong to one worker for its lifetime, and the one Parser is used by one worker at a time, in the order the token sets. **Internal locking lives at fan-in points and nowhere else:** the merger's mutex, taken once per batch, and the error record behind `fail`. There is no merger goroutine; `main` calls `Flush` after `Run` returns. Terms reach the merger in scheduling order, but `Flush` puts every level of the output in content order (§Output order).

Dependency direction: `ident` depends on nothing in the module, and `expr` only on `ident`; `intern` depends on `expr` and `ident`; `codec` depends on all three; `eval` depends on `expr` and `ident`; `contract` and `merge` sit on top of `eval`, and `merge` also uses `codec`'s Writer, `walk` on top of `contract`, and `worker` on top of everything. `ident` and `intern` own identity (§Identity); `expr` owns all value types (Expr tree, arithmetic ring, Scalar normal form, and converters); `walk` owns the Walker; `codec` owns JSON I/O and is the only package that knows Mathematica head names; `eval` owns the scalar arithmetic engine. Singleton-collapse and Expr-materialisation do not happen inside `expr`; the writer streams the output's monomials directly, picking 0/1/N for the output and for each monomial from a precomputed item count.

Naming: types ending in `Expr` (`PlusExpr`, `TimesExpr`, `PowerExpr`, `RationalExpr`, `ComplexExpr`, `HeadExpr`) disambiguate from same-named Mathematica heads and signal "implements `Expr`". `Atom` / `Symbol` / `Pair` / `Slot` / `Term` carry no such suffix because there is no Mathematica head to collide with.

## Why coefficients flow as `expr.Scalar`

`expr.Scalar` is the sum-of-monomials normal form (see `docs/scalar-normal-form.md`). Each `Term.Coeff` carries one. The flow:

1. **Normalise in the walk.** The parser has folded the numbers of each product the walk reaches into its coefficient (§Component Invariants > Parser). The walker takes each scalar factor as one term, whose value `eval.Folder.Fold` computes: an opaque factor is one monomial with that factor to the power 1, and a sum, a product or a positive power is computed from its parts' values with an `eval.Sum` and `Calculator.Mul`. The walker multiplies the coefficients of a product's digits and the product's own with `Calculator.MulTo`, into a buffer per digit, and `Next` returns each term, `expr.Term{Pairs, Coeff: scalar}`.
2. **Multiply through the traces in the contractor.** `NewContractor` assembles one trace per dimension as an `expr.Scalar` literal: `4` (`Complex{4, 0}`, no opaques), `D` (`OneComplex` × the parser's one node of `"D"`) and `-4 + D` (those two monomials) — trivial shapes that don't need the walk. Each contracted metric multiplies the worker's running coefficient by the trace of its dimension through its private `Calculator.Mul`. No further parsing of "what 4 means" — it's already in normal form.
3. **Accumulate in the merger.** The merger holds one `eval.Sum` per subtree behind its mutex. A worker's `Input.Add(terms)` keys each term of a batch outside the lock (`KeyedTerm.Set`: a hash of the Pair list's slots, and one of each monomial's word of opaque factors), then locks once for the batch, for `Sum.AddKeyed`, which adds each monomial's coefficient to the one stored under its word, or stores a copy of the monomial. There is no Walker at this stage — all walking happened upstream.
4. **Order on output.** A `Sum` returns its monomials in insertion order and drops those whose coefficients cancelled: a Scalar's monomials carry no order in flight. `Merger.Flush` orders them, with every other level of the output, by content (§Output order).

Memory is bounded by the distinct words seen (Pair list × monomial), not by total incoming terms, and by `-merge-memory` (§Component Invariants > Merger).

## Identity

Identity — whether two indices, momenta, opaque factors, Pairs or words of factors are the same — comes from keys that `ident` and `intern` define, never from rendered text, and keys never order output.

There are two kinds of key:
- **`ident.ID`, for the values the parser reads.** A run-scoped `ident.Table` keeps the key of every value keyed in the run. Two values get the same ID exactly when their contents are equal: a leaf by its content (a symbol by its name, an integer by its value, a non-integer number by its text), a composite by its kind and the IDs of its parts (`intern.Keys.Expr`). IDs follow arrival order, so they never order output. No value has the zero ID, which marks a node not yet keyed.
- **`ident.Hash`, for objects made after parsing:** a term's Pair list, from its slots, and a monomial's word of opaque factors, from their nodes' keys and exponents. It is built where the object is made, without the table, and is not unique: its user confirms a match against the parts, Pairs by value and opaque factors by node and exponent.

Within one context, two opaque factors, or two slots of one dimension, get equal keys exactly when they render identically, so the symbol `"3"` and the integer `3` differ, and two opaque factors merge exactly when their JSON is the same. A key mirrors how its value renders: a Pair's key lists its slots as the writer writes them, and a `Power` keys a cached exponent like the integer it renders as. The one exception: a parsed `Pair` and an unknown head named `Pair` inside an unknown head can render alike but key as kinds `Pair` and `Head`; which one a position holds follows from its ancestors, so the two never meet in one context.

Who keys what:
- **The parse stage** owns the run's only `intern.Keys` and is the only writer of the table. An index slot is keyed by its name's symbol; a momentum by its source as written (`Keys.Label`, which stores the source under the key), so `p + q` and `q + p` are two labels where they are not expanded. Neither key includes the dimension, since contraction lowers it. Once a summand is read, `Parser.walked` keys each opaque factor the walk reaches with `Keys.Opaque`, which returns the one node of its value, the first node keyed with its key, stored in the table under it. Walks run while parsing goes on and must not write the table, so these nodes exist before a summand is emitted. Any other node gets a key only when a key that contains it is needed, inside an opaque factor or a momentum source, and keeps it on the node.
- **`eval`** puts each opaque factor's shared node in its monomial, never building one; `eval.KeyedTerm.Set` hashes a term outside the merger's lock, and `eval.Sum` confirms each match.
- **The contractor** matches indices by `Slot.ID`, the name alone, and sorts the slots of a metric or a scalar product and the Pairs of a term by value, so that equal Pair lists are equal sequences. The `D` a trace yields is `Parser.Dimension()`, the parser's one `Symbol` for every `"D"`, so the two merge.

**Values made of IDs.** A slot is one `uint32` (`expr.Slot`): the ID shifted left past an index bit and the two bits of its `Dimension`, read by `ID`, `Index` and `Dim`, and lowered by `WithDim` without the table; the parser rejects an ID past `expr.MaxSlotID`. A `Pair` is its two slots in a `uint64`, index slots first, and is not an `expr.Expr`: every argument of a node is an `expr.Arg`, which holds a Pair inline or another node. Slots and Pairs hold no pointers, so the garbage collector does not scan their slices. The table holds what some IDs stand for: a symbol's name (`Table.Name`) and its one `Symbol`, a momentum's source and an opaque factor's one node (`Table.Value`). Walking and contraction never read it. The parse stage reads it back to expand momentum sources and share opaque factors; each merger `Input` reads names and sources while parsing goes on, to find a term's first Pair; and the writer and the output order read them in `Flush`. These reads take no lock: the table holds names and values in chunks that never move (`ident.column`), the parse stage sets each element once, and every ID a worker holds left the parser through a channel after its element was set.

The bug class to keep ruled out: a symbol must reach the table as a symbol. `parseAtom` makes every JSON string token a symbol, keyed by its name (`Keyer.Symbol`), and every non-integer number an `Atom` of its text; don't add a parser shortcut that bypasses it.

## Output order

The output order is a structural comparison of content, defined in `merge/order.go` and imposed by `Merger.Flush`. It never uses keys or arrival order. The writer writes what it is given, in the order given. From the inside out:
- **Leaves:** integers by value (`int32` and `*big.Int` alike), then non-integer numbers by their text, then symbols by the bytes of their names.
- **Composite nodes** come after leaves, ranked by kind: `Rational`, `Complex`, `Power`, `Times`, `Plus`, `Pair`, then unknown heads. Known kinds compare their children in order (`Power` its base, then its exponent, cached or not); unknown heads compare by head symbol, compound heads after every symbol, then by arguments. The inside of an opaque factor is compared, never reordered.
- **A slot:** an index by name, a momentum by source, then either by dimension: no label, then `D`, then `D-4` (sample 230).
- **A Pair:** metric, then vector, then scalar product; then its slots in order. The slots of a metric or a scalar product are sorted; a vector writes its index first.
- **A monomial's opaque factors** are sorted by node. The factors of one node, which only the int32 bound keeps apart, keep the order in which they were multiplied (a stable sort), so that the merger below the root merges what the root does (501). The numeric coefficient is not a factor for ordering; the writer puts it first.
- **A coefficient's monomials** by their opaque lists, compared as lists of copies, `x^n` as `n` copies of `x`: `x`, `x²`, `x²·y`, `x·y`, `x·y²` (498–500). `order.compareFactor` does it entry by entry: of two powers of one node, one that ends its monomial comes first, two that do by exponent ascending, and two that do not descending. Past the bound the entries are compared as they stand.
- **A term's Pairs** are sorted.
- **The addends of the root sum** by Pair list, then by opaque list, each compared as a list with a shorter prefix first. So the pair-free monomials come first, then the tensor terms by Pair list.

Two values compare equal only when they render identically, so the whole output is fixed by its content. The addend order is lexicographic over a word, the term's sorted Pairs, the end of the Pairs, then its sorted opaque factors as copies; the merger partitions terms by the first member of this word and flushes the partitions in member order, so it produces this order by construction (§Component Invariants > Merger).

## Component Invariants

### Parser (`codec/parser.go`, `codec/walked.go`)
- **One goroutine at a time, no mutex.** `ParseJson(decoder, emit func(expr.Arg) bool) error` is synchronous: each top-level summand is handed to the `emit` callback inline, and the parse stops before the next one once `emit` returns false. The caller decides where it runs; the parser starts no goroutine. Top-level `Plus` is split addend-by-addend at `ParseJson`'s root branch, so each addend reaches the Walker as a summand of its own — that's where multi-addend top-level inputs become parallel work.
- **Panic-based errors** (shared with Writer via `codec/errors.go`): inner helpers call the free functions `panicf(fmt, args)` / `assert(msg, err)` which panic with `wrappedError`. `ParseJson` installs `defer recoverWrapped(&err)` and returns the wrapped `error`. Do not add `error` returns to inner helpers.
- **Streaming, no intermediate `[]any`.** `parsePair` consumes `BeginArray + name` and dispatches directly to `parseLorentzIndex` / `parseMomentum`, then rejects a Pair whose two arguments differ in dimension. There is no `Object` materialization step.
- **`parseDimension` reads three labels:** none (`expr.Dim4`), the string `D` (`expr.DimD`), and `D-4`, which must arrive as exactly `["Plus", -4, "D"]` (`expr.DimDMinus4`, matched token by token in `parseEvanescent`). Any other label is rejected.
- **`Parser.walked` makes the part of a summand that the walk reaches,** once the summand is read, since a `Power`'s exponent, which decides whether its base is walked, arrives after the base. It descends the nodes that `expr.Expands` multiplies out and leaves the walk only those, numbers folded into coefficients, Pairs and opaque factors:
  - **Numbers fold into one coefficient per product.** The factors of a product that are numbers (`expr.ComplexFromExpr`), or that walk to one, multiply into `TimesExpr.Coeff`; a number standing alone is a product with only a coefficient. A positive power of a number folds by squaring (`Complex.Pow`). A product folds the factors written as numbers first, and once its coefficient is 0 it walks no other factor.
  - **A `Rational` or `Complex` that is read** becomes the product or sum it stands for: `Rational[a, b]` the product of `a` with the coefficient `b⁻¹`, or with the opaque factor `Power[b, -1]` when `b` is not a number; `Complex[a, b]` the sum of `a` and `i·b`.
  - **Momentum combinations expand.** `expandPair` reads each momentum source of a Pair from the table, and `distribute` replaces a Pair whose source is a `Plus`, or a `Times` of exact numbers and one other factor, with the sum it stands for; any other node is a label, keyed with `Keys.Label`, before the other slot is expanded the same way. Nothing is merged here.
  - **Factors are ordered by `Len`,** ascending and stably, by the length each counts in the product's `Len` (`expr.ChildLen`), so the last factor of a product has the most terms, and the walk streams it slowest.
  - **Opaque factors are shared:** any other node is replaced by the one node of its value (`Keys.Opaque`).

  walked rebuilds each node it changes through its constructor and reuses the written node's slices. It enters no opaque factor and no momentum source, so a node there keeps its written form: a `Times` keeps its input order and its numbers.
- **Buffer reuse is re-entrant**: `p.indices`, `p.momenta` and `p.sBuf` are `scratch` buffers (`codec/scratch.go`) shared by every level of the recursive parse, because a `Pair` can sit inside another `Pair`'s `Momentum` source (sample 170) and a sum or product inside anything. A user notes the buffer's length on entry, appends its items past it, then `take`s them or reads them in place and `truncate`s back. Never reset a scratch with `[:0]` in the middle of a parse. `ParseJson` truncates every scratch on exit.
- **Input validation**: `ParseJson` reads to the end of input, unless `emit` stops it, and rejects anything but whitespace after the expression; `parseAtom` rejects JSON objects, `true`, `false` and `null`, and an empty array is rejected wherever it appears. `openArray` rejects nesting deeper than `maxDepth` (10000 − 4): the writer places a part of the input at most four levels deeper than the input does, so whatever the parser accepts fits the encoder's limit, and a rejection always comes before any output.
- **`parseNamedExpr`** is the dispatch point. Handles `Plus`, `Times`, `Power`, `Pair`, `Rational` (→ `*expr.RationalExpr`), `Complex` (→ `*expr.ComplexExpr`), and anything else as an `*expr.HeadExpr` via `parseHead`, whose arguments parse like any expression. A `HeadExpr` is one opaque scalar factor (`Len` 1, `IsScalar` true), rendered as it came.
- **Inside an unknown head, tensor heads stay unknown heads.** `p.heads` counts the unknown heads around the current position; while it is non-zero, `Pair` goes to `parseHead` too, unchecked and in input order. A bare `LorentzIndex` or `Momentum` outside a `Pair` is a `HeadExpr` at any depth. While `p.heads` is non-zero, `openExpr` also accepts an expression as the head (`f[a][b]` is `[["f", "a"], "b"]`); elsewhere a head must be a symbol.
- **Leaves: `Symbol` and the Atom type map.** A JSON string token becomes the parser's one `*expr.Symbol` for that name, whose `Key` the table maps back to the name (including `D`, which `NewParser` makes first and `Parser.Dimension` hands the contractors; the contraction factor `4` is a `Complex` coefficient, not a leaf). Each number is an `Atom` of its own: an integer that fits `int32` in `Int`, an integer beyond int32 a `*big.Int` in `Big`, and a non-integer (floats, scientific notation) its raw text, a `jsontext.Value`, in `Text`. Both integers fold into the coefficient ring wherever the walk reaches them. `*RationalExpr`, `*ComplexExpr` and `*HeadExpr` are standalone `Expr` types, not leaves.
- **`parsePower` always returns `*PowerExpr`.** When the exponent parses as an `int32` Atom of any sign, the parser calls `expr.NewPowerExprInt(child, v)` — caches `Child + ExpInt`, leaves `Exp` the zero `Arg`. Otherwise (symbolic, compound, or any non-int32 literal such as a big integer or float), the parser calls `expr.NewPowerExpr(child, exp)` — sets `Child + Exp = exp`, leaves `ExpInt = 0`. The walk expands it when `ExpInt > 0` and takes it as one opaque factor otherwise. The writer always emits `["Power", child, exp]`. Zero-exponent simplification to `1` is intentionally NOT performed (`0^0` ambiguity, sample 40).
- **`Rational`/`Complex` parse via `parseBinaryArgs`,** as do a `Power`'s base and exponent, which rejects a count other than two. Any `Expr` is valid in numerator/denominator/Re/Im (e.g. `Rational[Plus[a,b], c]` works). `expr.NewRationalExpr` and `expr.NewComplexExpr` keep the parts as written and decide whether the node is read (`expr.Expands`): one that is not a number is, unless it has a tensor part (`IsScalar` false) or divides by an exact 0.

### Contractor (`contract/contractor.go`)
- **Per-worker, no shared state, no mutex.** Each worker has two `Contractor`s, one for its walker, which contracts the factors it multiplies out, and one for the batches it contracts into the merger. Never share across workers.
- **`addPair` is stateful across a single term's pairs**: `c.indexPairs` accumulates unmatched pairs during one call to `ContractAndNormalize`. It is implicitly drained (not reset) — pairs remaining in `indexPairs` after the loop are moved to `c.pairs`.
- **Contraction takes the meet of dimensions.** `addPair` matches indices by name and narrows the dimension of the Pair it builds, `dim &= oldPair.Dim()`, for each Pair it merges, then writes the meet into both slots of the result, so a `D`-dimensional momentum contracted with a 4-dimensional vector comes out 4-dimensional. When the meet is empty, 4 with `D-4`, `ContractAndNormalize` drains its index map and reports that the term vanished.
- **Owns one `*eval.Calculator` and pre-built traces**, one `expr.Scalar` per dimension, assembled once in `NewContractor`: `4`, `"D"` and `-4 + "D"`. The `"D"` opaque is the symbol `NewContractor` is given, `Parser.Dimension()`. A contracted metric folds the trace of its meet into `c.coeff` with `c.calc.Mul(c.coeff, c.traces[dim])`.
- **Output Pairs are the contractor's buffer**, `c.pairs`, valid until the next call; the caller reads or copies them before then. `term.Coeff` is `c.calc.Mul(term.Coeff, c.coeff)`, which may share slices with the input's coefficient, so it is valid as long as the input's is. Sharing is safe because nothing mutates a Scalar in flight and each `Sum` copies what it keeps.
- **Output pairs are sorted** by value, with the slots of a metric or scalar product in value order, so that equal Pair lists give equal keys. This is identity only: `Flush` re-sorts slots and Pairs by content for output.
- **Buffer reuse**: `c.pairs`, `c.indices`, `c.momenta` are cleared with `[:0]`. `c.coeff` is reset to `expr.OneScalar` (= multiplicative-identity Scalar). `c.indexPairs` is replaced by a fresh map after a term that may have put more than `eval.ScratchLimit` entries in it: a map never shrinks, and ranging over it costs its peak size.

### Walker (`walk/walker.go`) and Folder (`eval/folder.go`)
- **`walk.Walker` is a pull state machine.** `Walk(e)` starts a summand, and `Next()` returns its terms one at a time and reports when there are none. The state lives on a stack of frames that the walker reuses across summands: one for each tensor `Plus` it is inside, which holds its addends and the next one to enter, and on top a leaf, a `Pair` or a scalar, or a product. A product steps through the combinations of its digits, the first fastest and the last, the factor with the most terms, slowest. A digit is a set of rows, one of which it holds, or a stream: a walker of its own over one factor, walked again from the start each time a slower digit moves on. The product keeps after each digit the product of the coefficients from it on, computed with `Calculator.MulTo` into a buffer per digit, so a step multiplies coefficients again only up to the digit that moved. `Next` returns the term's Pairs and coefficient in the walker's buffers, valid until its next call.
- **`Walker.product` decides the digits,** in one place. The product's coefficient multiplies its digits'. A factor of one term is a digit of one row: a `Pair`, a scalar, whose value `eval.Folder` computes, or a tensor node of one term. A factor that closes an index name inside itself (`open`) can merge with itself, so it is multiplied out first, its rows merged. The factors are linked into groups (`group`) by the names they leave open, and those that leave none into one group, all but the factor streamed slowest. A group of several factors, one of which has more than one term, is one digit (`merge`): its members' rows multiplied together, or, where it holds a factor to stream, the group walked as a product on the walker's `sub`, contracted with the walker's own `Contractor` and merged in its `eval.Sum`. Every other factor is streamed. The factors multiplied out on their own and the streams start before any group is multiplied out, so a factor with no terms ends the product first. A tensor `Power[b, n]` is two digits (`power`): the rows of `n − 1` copies of `b`, multiplied in one at a time and merged after each, and the rows of one.
- **Exactness.** Contracting part of a term first gives the same result as contracting it whole, for input that uses each index name at most twice in a term: contraction joins the Pairs that share a name into chains and loops, and joining part of a chain first changes neither the chain nor the trace of its loop. For malformed input, the occurrences inside a part multiplied out first pair first (samples 126–129).
- **The walk takes a `Times`'s factors in the order the parser left them**, sorted by `Len`. Factor order reaches the result only through greedy contraction of malformed input (samples 07, 08, 34, 126).
- **`eval.Folder` computes the value of a scalar node.** `Fold(e)` expands exactly what `expr.Expands` says, of which walked leaves only sums, products and positive powers: a `Plus` adds its addends' values in a Sum, all of them folded before any is added; a `Times` multiplies its coefficient by its factors' values; `Power[b, n]` raises `b`'s value at once when it is one monomial whose exponents stay within int32, and otherwise multiplies it in `n − 1` times; both stop at zero. Any other node is one opaque factor, its node. `Expands` is shared with the parse stage, so a change to what is expanded belongs there, never in the folder's switch.
- **What the walk reads again.** A group is multiplied out once, when its product starts, and the slowest stream is walked once; every other stream is walked again for each combination of the slower digits. The walker changes nothing in the tree, and drops what a frame holds once the frame is done.
- **Ownership.** Each worker builds one Walker with its own Contractor (`walk.NewWalker(contract.NewContractor(d))`), calls `Walk` for each summand, then `Next` until it reports none. A walker's own walkers, its `sub` and the streams of its products, share its `Folder`, `Calculator` and `Contractor`, used one call at a time, and have a `Sum` each.

### Expr-side coefficient layer (Expr constructors / Writer)
- **`Expr` is minimal: `Len() int` and `IsScalar() bool`.** `Len` counts the terms of a node's expansion before anything merges, a scalar child of a tensor node counting 1, saturating at `math.MaxInt`; the parser orders a product's factors by it, the workers size their batches of summands by it, and `Walker.product` makes a factor of one term a row. `IsScalar` distinguishes coefficients from pair-bearing nodes. All other behavior (serialization, traversal, grouping) lives in dedicated types. Adding behaviour back to the interface is a regression — each new method either belongs on a dedicated type or is derivable from the existing two.
- **`NewPlusExpr` / `NewTimesExpr` / `NewPowerExprInt` / `NewPowerExpr`**, and **`NewRationalExpr` / `NewComplexExpr`**, are the only constructors. They compute and cache `len` / `scalar` from children, in time independent of a `Power`'s exponent, but do **no** singleton or empty-array collapse — `NewPlusExpr([])` yields a `*PlusExpr` with `len = 0`, `NewTimesExpr(c, [x])` yields a `*TimesExpr` with one child. The writer handles 0/1/N for the output and for each monomial at output time from a precomputed count. Parser-built `*PlusExpr` / `*TimesExpr` *Exprs* themselves render fixed-shape via `writeExpr → writeArray`.
- **Heap-allocated, persistent.** Expr values live for the lifetime of the run. Don't pool or reuse them. (Compare: the parser's `scratch` buffers, `c.pairs` in the contractor, the `eval.Buffer`s of the walker's product frames and the workers' batches, which are reused.)
- **Numbers fold in the parser, values in `eval`.** Coefficient arithmetic is performed against the exact Gaussian-rational ring (int32 fast path, `math/big` fallback — see Scalar normal form below): `Parser.walked` multiplies the numbers of each product into its coefficient and folds a positive power of a number, and `Calculator.Mul`, `Sum` and `Folder` compute the values of the rest. The Expr constructors deliberately skip folding. Adding a new arithmetic rule means touching `eval` or `Parser.walked` (and possibly `expr.Rational` / `expr.Complex`), never the Expr constructors.
- **`Writer` holds the encoder, the `ident.Table` and a reusable big-int scratch buffer**, so it is single-goroutine (the merger builds one per `Flush`). Two exported entry points, each `defer recoverWrapped(&err)`-protected, stream the whole output:
  - `Add(pairs []expr.Pair, m expr.Monomial) error` and `Close() error` — the merger's path, which hands over the output's monomials in output order, each with its term's Pairs. A pair-free monomial is an addend, and so is a tensor term, a run of monomials with equal Pairs. The output is `0 → 0`, `1 → the bare addend`, `N → ["Plus", addend...]`. The first addend is held until a second one begins, and each later tensor term until its second monomial, which opens `["Times", ["Plus"`; so the writer holds one whole tensor term at most.
  - An addend of one monomial is `writeMonomial(m, pairs...)`, which pre-computes its item count and picks `0 → 1`, `1 → bare`, `N → ["Times", ...]`, its Pairs written after its factors. A tensor term of several monomials is `["Times", ["Plus", monomial...], pair...]` (`openTimes`, `endAddend`).
  - `writeExpr(n expr.Expr)` — fixed-shape, no flatten and no identity collapse, an eight-arm type switch over `*Symbol`, `*Atom`, `*HeadExpr`, `*PowerExpr`, `*RationalExpr`, `*ComplexExpr`, `*TimesExpr`, `*PlusExpr`; names and momentum sources come from the table. Canonical Mathematica never nests Times-in-Times or Plus-in-Plus, so straight emission is canonical.
  - **Render-faithfully contract.** `writeMonomial` only suppresses its `Coeff` factor when `m.Coeff == expr.OneComplex`; the node of each opaque factor flows through `writeExpr` verbatim, from `writeFactor`, which wraps it in `["Power", node, n]` when its exponent `n` is above 1 (correct, per `docs/scalar-normal-form.md` §5b). **Open gap**: a parsed `Times[1, x]` embedded in an opaque sub-tree round-trips as `["Times", 1, x]` instead of `x`. The natural site to add collapse is `writeExpr`'s `*expr.TimesExpr` arm; restrict suppression to parsed Times children only, never a monomial's opaque factors.

### Scalar normal form (`pkg/pipeline/expr/scalar.go`, `pkg/pipeline/eval/`)
- **`expr.Scalar` is a sum of `expr.Monomial`s; each `Monomial` is `Complex` × `[]expr.Factor`.** A `Factor` is an opaque factor to a positive int32 power: `Node`, the one node of its value, so two are equal exactly when they are the same pointer, and `Exp`. `eval` keeps a monomial's factors sorted by key (`expr.KeyOf`) until the merger's flush, one entry per node: `mergeOpaques` adds the exponents of equal keys, unless the sum would pass the int32 range, where the two entries stay apart and nothing wraps.
- **`eval.Sum` groups monomials by word and folds Coeffs exactly.** `Complex.Add` / `Complex.Mul` never fail (the ring promotes to `math/big`), so adding a monomial whose word is present is a straight sum into the stored coefficient. `Sum.Scalar` and `Sum.Terms` drop zero-coefficient monomials (using `Complex.IsZero`) and leave the rest in insertion order. A Sum keeps a map for its next use only if it held at most `eval.ScratchLimit` entries.
- **`expr.Rational` and `expr.Complex` are exact (int32 fast path + `*big.Rat` fallback).** `Rational` maintains `den > 0` and `gcd(|num|, den) = 1`, and uses its unexported big field iff the value exceeds int32. `ComplexFromExpr` keeps int32 atoms and int32/int32 `*RationalExpr` (the shape real inputs carry) on the allocation-free path; only `*big.Int` operands touch `math/big`. `Add`/`Sub`/`Mul`/`Neg` return a plain `Rational` and never fail: the int32 path goes through `reduce64` / `finalize`, which promote to `big.Rat` past int32, and `bigRational` demotes a big result that fits back. Only `Div` (and `Complex.Inverse` / `Div`) carry an `ok` flag, solely for division by zero. Never compare two `Rational`/`Complex` for value-equality with `==` when a big value may be involved — only the `!= OneComplex` (vs. the small identity) and `IsZero` checks are used.
- **Every `Power` with `ExpInt > 0` expands,** so `Power[x, 3]` becomes the factor `x` with exponent 3, as `x·x·x` and `Times[x, x, x]` do, whether it stands alone or in a tensor product, where the walk takes it as one scalar factor. A Pair keeps one entry per occurrence.
- **`Scalar` exits via the writer's `Add` entry,** monomial by monomial. A coefficient of one monomial is inlined alongside the Pairs (`Times[Coeff_factors_of_m..., p1, p2]`, no intermediate Plus wrapping); a coefficient of several renders as `["Plus", monomial, ...]` inside the term's `Times`. `writeRational` reads the int32 fast path via `Rational.Num()` / `Den()`, emitting JSON tokens directly; big-valued Rationals are emitted as full-precision JSON numbers through a reusable scratch buffer (`big.Int.Append`, no per-call allocation).

### Merger (`merge/merger.go`)
- **Mutex-protected fan-in.** Each worker adds through its own `Input` (`Merger.NewInput`). `Input.Add(terms)` keys each term of a batch (`eval.KeyedTerm.Set`) and finds its member, outside the lock, then locks `m.mu` once for the batch to add each term to its subtree's `Sum`. Concurrency changes only the order in which terms are added: coefficients are exact, words match by content, and `Flush` orders every level by content. The merger has no goroutine of its own — `Flush` runs from `main` after `worker.Run` returns.
- **Subtrees by the first member of a term's word** (§Output order). The root keeps one subtree per first member: the term's first Pair in content order (`order.firstPair`), oriented, or 0, the end of the Pairs, for a pair-free term. Equal terms have equal members, so they meet in one subtree. Below the root, a merger made at flush keys its subtrees by the member one position deeper (`position.below`): the next Pair; after the end of the Pairs, each monomial on its own by its first opaque factor; then the next factor. A factor's member (`factorMember`) packs its key, whether it ends the monomial, and its exponent.
- **An `eval.Sum` per subtree.** A term's Pair list selects its group, and each monomial merges by its word within the group. The hashes are built from the term as given, so every term of a Sum must be in one canonical order: identity order at the root, as the contractor and `eval` leave them; content order below it, where each term read back from the file is sorted (`order.sort`) before it is keyed.
- **`Flush(enc *jsontext.Encoder)` materialises the output in content order,** one subtree at a time, in the order of their members (`order.compareMember`). Each word of a subtree precedes every word of a later one, so the subtrees' outputs follow one another in content order. For each subtree, `Sum.Terms` returns the groups without their cancelled monomials and empties the Sum (`Flush` is destructive). Flush sorts each group in place (`order.sort`), splits the pair-free group into one addend per monomial, sorts the addends and hands their monomials, in order, to `codec.Writer.Add`, then calls `Close`; the merger writes no JSON tokens itself. Sorting in place is safe because the merger's Sums copied every Pair list and opaque factor they keep, and the workers are done.
- **Moving subtrees to a file (`-merge-memory`).** With a limit `N > 0`, each Sum counts the approximate bytes it keeps (`Sum.Bytes`, without coefficients beyond int32), and when the merger holds more than `N` it moves every subtree that has grown since the last move to its temporary file (`merge/dump.go`): one segment per subtree, a record per term, each opaque factor as its key and exponent, which finds its node in the table again. The file is made on the first move and shared by every merger below the root. The end of a word, member 0 in the opaque factors, holds a single word, so it is never counted or moved, which ends the recursion below.
- **Merging moved subtrees again.** At flush, a merger that has moved terms first moves all it still holds. Then, subtree by subtree, a new merger one position deeper reads the subtree's segments, sorts each term in content order, adds it, may move terms to the end of the same file, and flushes into the same writer, recursively; afterwards the file is cut back to its size before the child. Equal words have equal members at every position, so they meet in one Sum however the terms were split between segments. The file's name is removed as soon as the file is made where the system allows that for an open file; elsewhere `Flush` removes the file, on errors too, and `main` calls `Merger.Close` when the run fails. An error on the file returns from `Input.Add`, which fails the run (§Pipeline), or from `Flush`.

## Walker design

The walk is a state machine with no callback: the worker pulls each term with `Next`, and the walker keeps its state on its own stack rather than in closures, so the worker can stop at any term.

A factor is taken in one of two ways. Streamed, it holds one of its terms at a time, and is walked again for each combination of the slower digits of its product. Multiplied out, it holds all its terms, merged and contracted as far as they close inside it. The walk multiplies out only what can merge: a factor that closes an index name inside itself, a group of factors that share the names they leave open, and the copies of a power. It streams every other factor, so a product of sums that share no names, nested at any depth, is never held whole. What merges inside a group merges before it meets the rest, so a chain of sums merges link by link rather than as the product of its links (sample 312). What merges only across a sum, such as a chain written in Horner form, is walked term by term ([known-limitations.md](known-limitations.md) §Walk memory and time). The rule is `Walker.product`; another algorithm replaces it there.

## Naming convention for strategy interfaces

Strategy interfaces use Go-idiomatic role nouns, not Java-style suffixes. The closest stdlib analogues — `go/ast.Walk(v Visitor, node Node)`, `path/filepath.WalkDir`, `database/sql.Driver` — name the strategy with a noun describing what it *is* in the algorithm's vocabulary. Avoid `Delegate` / `Listener` / `Strategy` / `Handler` for new strategy interfaces.
